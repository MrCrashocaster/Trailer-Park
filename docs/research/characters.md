## Relationship graph: interpretation

The central trio works because affection survives incompatible priorities. Julian seeks control, Ricky pushes immediate action, and Bubbles often wants safety and belonging. Their roles shift: none is exclusively leader, fool, or conscience.

Lahey and Randy also form an emotionally consequential relationship rather than merely a pair of obstacles. Barbara links authority to ownership and personal history. Lucy, Sarah, Trinity, and Ray connect schemes to households and obligations. Helpers and rivals extend the consequences beyond the central trio.

## Lessons for an original ensemble

Distribute mechanical competence across residents. Give each person a goal unrelated to the players, someone they care about, a boundary, and a practical asset. Let relationships change who will help, testify, forgive a debt, or share a resource.

Do not recreate the trio with changed names, or pair a recognizable drink-carrying planner with a malapropism-heavy grower and a bespectacled cat caretaker. Likewise, avoid a recognizable supervisor/assistant pair with the same appearance, habits, and relationship beats. Broad ensemble principles can inform a substantially different cast.
